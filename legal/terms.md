BillTracking republishes the official legislative record of the US Congress and the European Union. It is **free**, there is nothing to buy, and it is run by one person, not a company. These terms are short because the arrangement is simple.

^The basics
## What BillTracking is

Automated feeds and an app that report steps in the legislative process, taken from official sources — Congress.gov, the European Parliament's open data, the Publications Office and the OEIL Legislative Observatory. A summary, when a post carries one, is the legislature's own official summary, quoted word for word under its source's name; we do not write or edit summaries. A summary is a short description, not the bill: it can simplify or leave out details that matter, and it describes one version of the bill, which may since have changed. The procedural facts, dates and vote tallies in a post's own lines are copied from the record.

>! **This is not legal advice, and it is not the official record.** It is a convenience layer over public sources. Every post links to its source. **If a decision matters — legally, professionally, financially — read the source, not us.**

### Errors

Automatically classifying legislative stages is genuinely difficult, and the record itself is sometimes amended after the fact. Matching a summary to the right version of a bill is done automatically too, and can go wrong. How we do it is on [Accuracy & corrections](accuracy.html). We do not promise accuracy; we promise that errors are corrected in the open and logged publicly. See [Accuracy & corrections](accuracy.html), and please report anything that looks wrong to [accuracy@billtracking.org](mailto:accuracy@billtracking.org).

Keywords and notifications are a convenience. A keyword is an exact match: it finds its words, whole and in the order you typed them (capital letters, accents and punctuation aside), in the parts of a post your keyword settings include, and, for a post that went out without an official summary, it can also find them in the bill's most recent official summary at that time. It does not match other forms of a word or synonyms, so it will not catch every bill about a topic, and a notification can arrive late or not at all. If you must not miss something, follow the bill and check the source.

### Availability

It is a free service run by one person on free infrastructure. It may be interrupted, delayed, or discontinued. If an upstream source changes or goes down, the feeds may go quiet. Please do not build anything you depend on around its continued availability without talking to us first.

^If you sign in
## Accounts and voting

- **Reading never requires an account.** An account exists so that a vote on a bill counts once per person.
- **One account per person.** Do not create multiple accounts to vote more than once, and do not use automated means to influence the counts. It corrupts the only thing the counts are for.
- **Votes are reader sentiment, not a poll.** They are a show of hands among whoever happens to use this app — self-selected, unweighted and unrepresentative. Please do not present them as public opinion research, and be careful citing them as anything more than what they are.
- **Read past the summary.** A summary is short and describes one version of a bill. Every post links to its official source; read it before you rely on what a bill does.
- **You may withdraw a vote, or all of your votes, or delete your account at any time** — from the You tab in the app, from your [account page](https://billtracking.org/account) on this website, or via the [deletion page](delete-account.html) and the [delete some of your data](delete-data.html) page.
- **We may remove votes we believe are not genuine**, cast by software or through accounts created to inflate a count, and may suspend the accounts involved.

How votes are stored and published is set out on the [privacy page](privacy.html). In short: who voted which way is never published, shown or attached to a profile.

^Using the content
## Content and reuse

The underlying legislative record is public and is not ours. US federal government works are not subject to copyright (17 U.S.C. § 105); EU documents are reusable under the European Commission's reuse decision and the European Parliament's reuse notice. Where you need the authoritative version, go to [api.congress.gov](https://api.congress.gov), the OEIL Legislative Observatory, or the EU Publications Office directly.

Our own writing, design and code are ours. **Teachers and students: use it.** Quote it, screenshot it, put it in a lecture, build a class exercise on it — that is what it is for, and there is a page of [ready-made exercises](educators.html). A credit and a link is all we ask. If you want a bulk dataset, write to us rather than scraping.

Do not use the service to break the law, to attack the infrastructure, or to misrepresent what it says.
