Terms of use

Using BillTracking

BillTracking republishes the official legislative record of the US Congress and the European Union. It is free, there is nothing to buy, and it is run by one person, not a company. These terms are short because the arrangement is simple.

The basics

What BillTracking is

BillTracking is a set of automated feeds and an app that report steps in the legislative process. The information comes from official sources: Congress.gov, the European Parliament's open data, the Publications Office and the OEIL Legislative Observatory. When a post has a summary, it is the legislature's own official summary, quoted word for word under its source's name. We do not write or edit summaries. A summary is a short description, not the bill. It can simplify or leave out details that matter, and it describes one version of the bill, which may have changed since. The procedural facts, dates and vote tallies in a post's own lines are copied from the record.

This is not legal advice, and it is not the official record. It is a convenience built on public sources, and every post links to its source. If a decision matters, for example legally, professionally or financially, read the source, not us.

Errors

Classifying legislative steps automatically is hard, and the record itself is sometimes changed after the fact. Matching a summary to the right version of a bill is also done automatically and can go wrong. Accuracy & corrections explains how we do it. We do not promise accuracy. We do promise that errors are corrected in the open and logged publicly. Please report anything that looks wrong to accuracy@billtracking.org.

Keywords and notifications are a convenience. A keyword is an exact match. It finds its words whole and in the order you typed them, ignoring capital letters, accents and punctuation, in the parts of a post your keyword settings include. For a post that went out without an official summary, it can also find them in the bill's most recent official summary at that time. It does not match other forms of a word or synonyms, so it will not catch every bill on a topic. A notification can also arrive late or not at all. If you must not miss something, follow the bill and check the source.

Availability

BillTracking is a free service run by one person on free infrastructure. It may be interrupted, delayed or shut down. If a source we read from changes or goes down, the feeds may go quiet. Please talk to us before you build anything you depend on around it staying available.

If you sign in

Accounts and voting

  • You don't need an account to read. An account is what lets each person vote once on a bill.
  • One account per person. Do not create extra accounts to vote more than once, and do not use automated means to influence the counts. That would make the counts meaningless.
  • Votes show reader sentiment. They are not a poll. They are a show of hands among whoever happens to use the app, so they are self-selected, unweighted and not representative. Please do not present them as public opinion research, and be careful citing them as anything more than that.
  • Read beyond the summary. A summary is short and describes one version of a bill. Every post links to its official source, so read that before you rely on what a bill does.
  • You may withdraw a vote, or all your votes, or delete your account at any time. You can do this from the You tab in the app, from your account page on this website, or through the deletion page and the delete some of your data page.
  • We may remove votes we believe are not genuine (cast by software, or through accounts made to inflate a count), and we may suspend the accounts involved.

The privacy page explains how votes are stored and published. In short, we never publish who voted which way, show it to anyone or attach it to a profile.

Using the content

Content and reuse

The legislative record itself is public and is not ours. US federal government works are not subject to copyright (17 U.S.C. § 105). EU documents can be reused under the European Commission's reuse decision and the European Parliament's reuse notice. When you need the authoritative version, go straight to api.congress.gov, the OEIL Legislative Observatory or the EU Publications Office.

Our own writing, design and code are ours. Teachers and students are welcome to use it. Quote it, screenshot it, put it in a lecture or build a class exercise on it. That is what it is for, and there is a page of ready-made exercises. All we ask is a credit and a link. If you want a bulk dataset, write to us instead of scraping.

Do not use the service to break the law, to attack the infrastructure, or to misrepresent what it says.

Legal

Liability and your rights

The service is provided as-is. To the extent the law allows, we are not liable for losses arising from your use of it. This includes decisions made on the basis of a post or a summary that turned out to be wrong or late.

What we do not exclude, because we cannot and should not: liability for death or personal injury caused by negligence, for fraud, for damage caused intentionally, and any liability that the law of your country does not allow to be excluded. If you are a consumer in the EU or UK, nothing here takes away rights you have under your own national law, including the right to bring a claim in your own courts.

We may update these terms. If we change something that matters, we will say so on this page and update the date. If you keep using the service after a change, that means you accept it. If you don't want to, you can stop using it and delete your account.
